H4

Student-t degrees of freedom

Degrees of freedom of a Student-t fitted to the z of H3: how heavy the tail is

Takeaway
  • A Student-t fitted to the z of Huchra's list has ν = 2.30 (1.91–2.98) against Planck and 1.97 (1.62–2.51) against Riess: a far heavier tail than a normal distribution (ν → ∞).
  • Table A2 gives 2.30 and 1.69, the same order as the edition-pair ν of the physical-constants chapter (1.00) and Bailey's (2017) 1.81.
Definition
The degrees of freedom ν and scale s of a zero-centered Student-t fitted by maximum likelihood to the z of H3. A smaller ν means a heavier tail (a normal distribution has ν → ∞). Fitted separately for each reference.
Formula
z ~ s · t(ν), ν and s by maximum likelihood. Rows with z = 0 are left out (the likelihood diverges at scale 0). 95% intervals: year-cluster bootstrap, 2,000 resamples.
Denominator
The same rows as H3: 548 in Huchra's list, 163 in Table A2 (162 against Planck).
How to read it
For Huchra's list ν = 2.30 against Planck (95% interval 1.91–2.98) and 1.97 against Riess (1.62–2.51). Table A2: 2.30 (1.53–12.21) and 1.69 (1.26–2.60). The edition-pair ν of the physical-constants chapter (M4) is 1.00; Bailey (2017) found ν = 1.81 ± 0.15 for pairs of individual measurements of fundamental constants.
Caveats
A ν near 2 is a tail whose variance is undefined. The Planck interval of Table A2 is open upward (1.53–12.21): 163 rows do not pin the tail down. The limits of H3 (choice of reference, dependence, unstated error levels) apply unchanged.
