M2

Edition-to-edition exceedance

Share of moves between consecutive editions not covered by the two uncertainties

Takeaway
  • Moves between consecutive editions exceeded twice the combined standard uncertainty of the two editions (|z| > 2) in 12.9% of pairs, about once in 8. The normal expectation is 4.55% (once in 22): what correct error bars would give.
  • Moves beyond three times (|z| > 3) occurred in 9.1% (about once in 11), far more often than the normal 0.27% (once in 370).
  • |z| > 1, by contrast, is rarer than the normal expectation: editions share data, so values mostly stay put and then move a long way at once when a new measurement or a corrected calculation arrives.
Definition
For each constant, the editions in which it is measured are put in order and each is paired with the next (an edition in which the constant is exact or absent is skipped). z is the change in value divided by the combined standard uncertainty √(u_old² + u_new²). z = 2 means a move of twice the combined width of the two error bars. The shares with |z| above 1, 2 and 3 are counted.
Formula
z = (x_new − x_old) / √(u_old² + u_new²). If the two editions were independent and normal, |z| > 1 would occur 31.7% of the time, >2 4.55%, >3 0.27%.
Denominator
132 consecutive pairs over 17 core constants: 96 four-year pairs from 1998 on, 36 earlier pairs (4 to 13 years apart).
How to read it
|z| > 2 occurs in 12.9% of pairs (95% interval 9.4–16.3%), about once in 8. The normal expectation is 4.55% (once in 22), what correct, independent uncertainties would give. |z| > 3 occurs in 9.1% (5.1–13.3%), about once in 11, far above the normal 0.27% (once in 370). |z| > 1, by contrast, occurs in 26.5% (16.7–37.1%), below the normal 31.7%. Small moves are rare and large ones common.
Caveats
Consecutive editions share much of their experimental data, so they are not independent. Without new data a value barely moves (10 pairs did not change in any printed digit), pulling z toward zero. That is why the low share of |z| > 1 cannot be read as generous uncertainties (wide error bars). The share of large moves exceeds the normal expectation despite this. Intervals are a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole constants (the pairs of one constant form one cluster; clusters are picked again at random, so one may come up twice and another not at all, the calculation is repeated, and the middle 95% of the results is the interval). With three candidates that meet the same selection rule added (helion and triton relative atomic masses, deuteron magnetic moment), the 20 constants give |z| > 2 in 12.1% and |z| > 3 in 8.1%.
