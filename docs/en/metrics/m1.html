M1

Relative-uncertainty half-life

Years for the relative uncertainty to halve

Takeaway
  • Among the 16 core constants whose uncertainty declined, the median relative uncertainty halved every 5.4 years.
  • The pace differs widely, from 3.0 years for the electron relative atomic mass to 12.2 years for the Newtonian constant of gravitation.
Definition
For each constant, log₂ of the relative standard uncertainty u/|x| is regressed on edition year. The half-life is minus the reciprocal of the slope.
Formula
log₂(u/|x|) = a + b·year, half-life = −1/b (years). The 95% interval maps b ± t(0.975, n−2)·SE(b) through the same expression (a regression interval, not a bootstrap). A slope flatter than −0.001 per year (half-life above 1,000 years) is reported as no decline.
Denominator
Constants with a measured (not exact) value in at least four editions: 17 core constants, 28 including display constants.
How to read it
The median half-life over the 16 core constants whose uncertainty declined is 5.4 years (5.7 over all 17, counting the one with no decline). The shortest is electron relative atomic mass, 3.0 years; the longest Newtonian constant of gravitation, 12.2 years. The neutron magnetic moment kept almost the same uncertainty from 1986 to 2022 (slope −7×10⁻¹¹ per year, interval including zero) and is shown as 'no decline'.
Caveats
Editions are unevenly spaced (4 to 13 years). Editions in which a constant is exact (h and k from 2018) are excluded. A straight line is a summary; constants whose uncertainty falls in steps, when a new method enters, have a low R².
