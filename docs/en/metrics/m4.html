M4

Tail heaviness

Student-t degrees of freedom fitted to edition-to-edition z

Definition
A zero-centred Student-t is fitted by maximum likelihood to the z (M2) of consecutive pairs whose value changed. The smaller ν, the heavier the tails (ν = 1 is the Cauchy distribution; ν → ∞ the normal).
Formula
z ~ s · t_ν, ν and s by maximum likelihood; 95% intervals from 2,000 cluster-bootstrap resamples of constants. Pairs whose printed value did not change (z = 0) are left out.
Denominator
122 of the 132 consecutive pairs over 17 core constants: those whose value changed.
How to read it
ν = 1.00 (95% interval 0.75–1.54), scale s = 0.38 (0.20–0.71). Pairs from 1998 on alone give ν = 0.92 (0.69–1.30). A ν near 1 means tails close to the Cauchy distribution. For reference, Bailey (2017) obtained ν = 1.81 ± 0.15 from pairs of individual measurements of seven constants.
Caveats
With the z = 0 pairs kept, the likelihood of a zero-centred Student-t grows without bound as s → 0 (for ν below 0.08), so no maximum-likelihood estimate exists; those 10 pairs are left out and counted in M2 instead. Bailey compares individual experimental results; this site compares recommended values across editions. Recommended values are weighted averages from which some results are dropped or whose uncertainties are expanded, so the two ν are not the same quantity. A scale below 1 reflects the data shared between editions (see M2). The sample is small and the intervals wide.
