Before CODATA — recommended values 1929–1969 and the Henrion & Fischhoff indices

Recommended values of the physical constants existed before CODATA. Birge (1929, 1941) and DuMond and Cohen (1950–1963) gathered the measurements of their day into adopted values with stated errors. This chapter transcribes those values for five constants (α⁻¹, h, e, mₑ, N_A) from the original tables and counts, against the 1973 and 2022 editions, whether the stated errors covered the later values.

Takeaway
  • Set against the 1973 edition, the 40 values of the five constants in eight reviews from 1929 to 1969 give a surprise index (|z| > 2.326) of 57.5% (23 values) and an interquartile index of 22.5% (9 values) (normal: 2% and 50%). Henrion & Fischhoff's Table I gives 57% and 22% for 40 values: the same counts with the decimals dropped. One of the eight, the 1957 book, reprints the 1955 adjustment unchanged.
  • Against the 2022 edition the surprise index is 60.0% and the interquartile index 15.0%. That more than half of the later values lie outside the stated 98% interval holds whichever edition is the reference.
  • Only the Birge ratio R_B (Henrion & Fischhoff's eq. (1), R_B² = Σz²/(N−1)) differs: 4.35 against the 7.42 of Table I. No sensitivity variant matches R_B, IQ and SI of Table I together, so the cause of the difference is not known.
  • Birge's 1929 h and e were 1.2% and 0.7% below the 2022 values: 6.7 and 4.7 times the standard uncertainty obtained from his probable errors.

Recommended values of five constants, 1929–2022

Timeline — who issued recommended values, 1923–1973

1923, 1926
The International Critical Tables, a project IUPAC approved in 1919, adopted values of the fundamental constants, prepared under the direction of Frederick E. Fowle of the Smithsonian Institution, in 1923 and printed them as a table in volume 1 in 1926, with no references, evaluation or discussion.
1926
Birge, in Science, pointed out inconsistencies in those values, sources of error and the interdependencies of the constants.
1928
The U.S. National Research Council set up a Committee on Physical Constants, chaired by Birge, who produced most of its output himself.
1929
Birge's first comprehensive review appeared as the first article in volume 1 of Reviews of Modern Physics; he updated it in two papers in 1941. These are the first two reviews in this chapter.
1940–1965
The NRC set up a Committee of Fundamental Constants and Conversion Factors. DuMond and Cohen produced major updates in 1947, 1950, 1952, 1955, 1959 and 1965; the 1950–1963 values in this chapter come from this series.
1957
Cohen, Crowe and DuMond, The Fundamental Constants of Physics (Interscience). Its Table 8-7 reprints the 1955 adjustment unchanged.
1965
The Cohen & DuMond review. As Cohen recalled in 1981, a value of the fine-structure constant from the fine structure of the hydrogen optical spectrum then differed by 22 parts per million from one derived from the microwave hyperfine separation (hydrogen maser), well outside the expected uncertainties. Even so, the review's values were endorsed by IUPAP and recommended for general use by IUPAC.
1966, 1969
The International Council of Science established CODATA in 1966 and its Task Group on Fundamental Constants in 1969. That year Taylor, Parker and Langenberg published an independent review built on their Josephson-effect measurement of e/h (this site's 1969 edition). Cohen wrote that it showed clearly that the fine-structure data were in error, and that the source of the error had not been identified by 1981.
1973
Cohen and Taylor produced the first CODATA recommended values. The later editions are covered in the other chapters of this site.

Cohen ended the same note: “It is perhaps ironic that the numerical values of the fundamental physical constants should change as often as they have over the past three decades, but it is the seeking for ultimate truth, rather than the attainment of the goal, that is important.”

Sources: Burgess, J. Phys. Chem. Ref. Data 44, 030401 (2015), sec. 3 (pp. 030401-2 to -3); E. R. Cohen, "This Week's Citation Classic", Current Contents no. 35 (Aug. 31, 1981); title page of the 1957 book (HathiTrust copy). The 22 ppm in the 1965 entry and its reading are Cohen's recollection, not a value recomputed on this site.

Henrion & Fischhoff (1986), recomputed

The "Recommended values" row of Table I in M. Henrion, B. Fischhoff, Am. J. Phys. 54, 791 (1986) (second to last, p. 794) reports R_B 7.42, IQ 22% and SI 57% (n = 40); the text (p. 795) says five constants in "eight reviews from 1929 to 1969" were set against the 1973 values. Probable errors were multiplied by 1.48 (p. 797, note 21). They did not list the eight, but the seven reviews their references point to (refs. 3, 17, 19, 28, 29: 1929, 1941, 1952, 1955, 1957, 1963, 1969) plus the 1950 letter, which gives all five constants, make eight. Below are the indices recomputed for these eight with their definitions, and sensitivity variants that change one condition at a time. IQ and SI give the same counts as Table I (which drops the decimals); only R_B differs. Leaving N_A on its original scale raises R_B to 6.59, and leaving probable errors unconverted to 5.68, but IQ and SI then no longer match. Counting each adjustment once (without 1957) gives R_B 4.36, IQ 25.7% and SI 54.3%.
