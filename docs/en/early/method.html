Before CODATA — method and limits

Takeaway
  • Values were transcribed by hand from page images of the original tables; an independent reader who did not know them read the same pages again, and all 30 agree.
  • The ± means different things: probable error in 1929, 1941 and 1950, standard error in 1952 and 1955, standard deviation from 1963. Probable errors are multiplied by 1.4826 to give standard uncertainties.
  • The atomic-weight scale behind the Avogadro constant moved from chemical to physical to unified (¹²C); values are put on the unified scale with the factors of Cohen & DuMond (1965).

What was transcribed

The five constants of the "Recommended values" row of Henrion & Fischhoff's (1986) Table I — α⁻¹, h, e, mₑ, N_A — as each paper gave them as the result of its adjustment (adopted values, least-squares adjusted output values). Where a paper prints more than one version (in 1929 a spectroscopic and a deflection electron mass), the one the paper used to compute other constants (spectroscopic) is taken. Guard digits printed below the line are used as part of the value, as the papers explain.

What the ± means, in the papers' words

  • 1929, Birge: "The real probable error in each constant is of course that indicated in the table" (p. 61).
  • 1941, Birge: the note to Table c explains how to compute "the probable error in a derived quantity" (Rep. Prog. Phys. p. 129; Rev. Mod. Phys. p. 237). No general defining sentence was found.
  • 1950, DuMond & Cohen: "the probable errors being all computed by the ellipsoid method" (p. 555).
  • 1952, DuMond & Cohen: "The quantity following each ± sign is the standard error by external consistency" (p. 706).
  • 1955, Cohen et al.: "The quantity following each ± sign is the standard error" (p. 378, Table XIII).
  • 1963, Cohen & DuMond: "the standard deviation error in the final digits" (p. 590, Table XVII).

A probable error is the half-width of the central 50% of a normal distribution, so standard uncertainty = probable error ÷ Φ⁻¹(0.75) ≈ probable error ÷ 0.6745; the calculation uses 1/Φ⁻¹(0.75) = 1.4826022. Henrion & Fischhoff used 1.48; the difference hardly moves the indices (sensitivity table).

Units and atomic-weight scales

  • For e the electromagnetic-unit (emu) figure is used where printed (1 emu = 10 C), because an esu figure depends on the speed of light of the day. 1950 prints esu only, converted with the same table's c (299 790.2 km/s).
  • h from erg s to J s, mₑ from g to kg.
  • N_A: the old chemical scale (O = 16) in 1929 and 1941, the physical scale (¹⁶O = 16) in 1950–1955, the unified scale (¹²C = 12) from 1963. Values are divided by "mass on physical scale / mass on unified scale = 1.000317917 ± 0.000000017" (Cohen & DuMond 1965, p. 547) and "old chemical scale / unified scale = 1.000043 ± 0.000005" (p. 548). The 1929 N₀ line states no scale; the chemical scale is inferred from the definition of atomic weights in Table a (O = 16.0000). Applying the 1965 chemical-scale factor to 1929 and 1941 is an assumption. Both factors are used as exact numbers and their uncertainty (at most 5 ppm) is not propagated; propagating it would enlarge the N_A uncertainties by less than 0.02% and leave the indices unchanged to two decimals.

Indices

z = (reference value − earlier value) / earlier standard uncertainty; the reference's uncertainty is not included (Henrion & Fischhoff's definition). The interquartile index (IQ) is the share with |z| < 0.6745, the surprise index (SI) the share with |z| > 2.326 (written 0.675 and 2.33 by Henrion & Fischhoff). The Birge ratio follows Henrion & Fischhoff's eq. (1) (p. 792), R_B² = Σz²/(N−1). Their equation is written with residuals about the group mean, but for the recommended values they state that residuals were taken relative to the 1973 estimates (p. 795), so z is used as it is. The root mean square √(Σz²/N) is given too. The 1969 values are the site's 1969 edition.

Checks

  • The 30 values (six papers from 1929 to 1963 × 5) were transcribed from page images; an independent reader who did not know them read the same pages from images again. Values, ±, exponents and kinds of ± agree 30/30.
  • The five values of the 1957 book (HathiTrust public-domain copy, Table 8-7, p. 267) were read from the page image and checked against HathiTrust's text layer. All five values and their ± equal Table XIII of the 1955 paper.
  • The two 1941 papers of Birge (Rep. Prog. Phys. and Rev. Mod. Phys.) give the same five values.
  • The ppm deviations by year broadly match Henrion & Fischhoff's Fig. 3 (1952–1973): for example 1952 h −147 ppm and N_A +126 ppm against 1973.

Limits

  • Henrion & Fischhoff did not list their eight reviews. The reviews identifiable from the references of Table I are seven, 1929, 1941, 1952, 1955, 1957, 1963 and 1969; they cite the 1950 letter only for their speed-of-light figure. The main set of eight adds 1950 to these, the combination for which IQ and SI equal Table I; the list itself is an inference. Because the 1957 book reprints the 1955 values, the same adjustment is counted twice. The seven without 1957 and the seven without 1950 are in the sensitivity table.
  • The reviews reuse the same experimental data and are not independent; 1952 and 1955 reanalyze the same 1952 data.
  • The 1973 and 2022 values are not the true values either.
  • The tables were published in journals; the site gives only the values (numbers) and pages, not the PDFs.
