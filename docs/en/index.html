Physical Constants Metrics

How often did the error bars that scientists published miss the next edition's value? This site joins the nine editions of the CODATA recommended values of the fundamental physical constants (1973–2022) and the 1969 adjustment that preceded them, and counts how values and uncertainties changed from edition to edition.

Takeaway
  • Uncertainties shrank more than ten-thousand-fold in half a century, yet moves between editions exceeded twice the published uncertainty 2.8 times as often as a normal distribution allows, and three times 34 times as often.
  • 29% of earlier ±2σ error bars do not contain today's (2022) value, with no clear difference between older and recent editions.
  • These numbers are the share of moves the reported uncertainties did not cover; the cause (overconfidence, systematic error, adjustment procedure) is not identified by these data.

Uncertainties shrank by more than four orders of magnitude in half a century. Yet the moves between editions often exceeded what those uncertainties allow. This site publishes those rates by constant and by edition, next to the earlier studies that asked the same question (Henrion & Fischhoff 1986; Bailey 2017). Every number carries its sample size and a 95% interval. "Scientists were overconfident" is an interpretation that would require looking into the measurements themselves, so the metric pages do not use it; the reading edition takes it up with its qualifications.
